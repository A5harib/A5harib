Accuracy notes

A few facts in the document were simplified or are out of date. Each was kept, but phrased correctly:

Moon phasesThe document says phases happen "when Earth blocks some of the sunlight". That describes an eclipse, not phases. The video uses the correct, simple idea from the same paragraph: the Moon orbits Earth and we see different amounts of its sunlit side.
Moons per planetThe document says "each planet has one or two moons". Mercury and Venus have none, and Jupiter and Saturn have dozens, so the video says "many planets have moons".
Number of moonsThe document gives 336 moons "as of 2009". Astronomers have found many more since then, so the video says "hundreds of moons" and stays correct as the count grows.
Shooting starsThe document calls a shooting star "a meteorite". The video explains that a shooting star is a small space rock glowing in the sky, and that a rock which lands on Earth is a meteorite. This matches the document's own Unit 4 definition.
Space shuttleThe shuttle stopped flying in 2011, so it is described in the past tense ("could blast off... came back").
Big DipperThe video calls it "part of a star picture called the Great Bear", which is more exact than calling the Big Dipper a constellation.
Added safety lineOne line was added: "never look straight at the Sun."
YouTube videosThe two linked videos cannot be embedded. Their purpose (a solar system song) is covered by the original sunshine song, the planet count-along and the Solar System Day sing-along. Teachers can still play the links in class.
